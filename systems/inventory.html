Core System

Inventory Management.

Oneway Inventory tracks stock levels, orders, sales and deliveries across your supply chain, so your business has the right amount of goods in the right place at the right time.

Replaces MarketMan or Restaurant365.

And it does what they can't on their own, because every System here shares one set of records.

  • A dish sold takes its ingredients

    A dish rung up at the register takes each of its ingredients off the shelf, costed from the invoices.

  • A repair takes its parts

    Parts are held for the work order and taken when it is done; their cost lands on the repair and on the thing it fixed.

  • Every item is a thing you own

    Stock lives in Asset Management's register, on the same map as everything else.

Part of Business OS, by contact, in the Closed Selective Server Beta. What is still being built is marked Planned.

Stock levels

The right amount, in every place.

  • Items, packs and variants

    A unit, pack sizes, a SKU and a barcode; sizes and colours each counted on their own.

  • By place and by shelf

    Stock where it actually sits, in every place the business keeps it.

  • Counts

    A whole place or item by item; the difference shown at once, booked by an owner.

  • Waste with a reason

    Spoiled, damaged, expired, lost or used in house, each in dollars.

  • One history

    Every change kept for good; on hand is never typed over.

  • Lots and expiry

    Each delivery's lot and the day it expires; the soonest used first, what expires this week said.

  • A count sheet, by scanning

    A whole place counted on one screen, a scanner or the phone's camera adding each one; labels printed.

Orders and deliveries

Ordered before it runs out, received as it comes.

  • Par and reorder points

    Per place or for the whole business; owners told once when it runs low.

  • A demand forecast

    When each item will run out at today's pace, and when to order.

  • Orders drafted for you

    What is low, onto its supplier's order, filled to par and rounded to the pack.

  • Approved and on its way

    An owner approves the order and marks it sent; open orders are listed until they arrive.

  • Deliveries received

    Received in part or in full, at the invoice's cost; what never came is said.

  • Suppliers measured

    Their stated lead time beside the real one, fill rate and on-time count.

  • Deliveries due and late

    Each order due from its supplier's days; the late ones first, in the room's Now.

  • Sending orders to suppliers

    A purchase order by email or PDF.

Sales

Every sale takes its stock.

  • Sales from Square

    Sales pulled from Square take stock by SKU, line by line.

  • The Shop and pickup orders

    Held while the order waits, taken when it is paid or picked up.

  • Sold out, everywhere

    A product at zero shows Sold out in the Shop and the App, and returns with new stock.

  • Other points of sale

    Shopify POS, Clover, Toast and Lightspeed sales taking stock.

    Planned

  • Recipes and plate cost

    A dish sold takes its ingredients; its cost from the invoices, its food cost against its price.

  • Products for sale

    An item offered in the Shop and the App, hidden until a person publishes it.

Across places

One supply chain, every place in it.

  • Moved between places

    From one place and shelf to another, both halves in one step.

  • Property to property

    Sent to another property of the same business, found there by its SKU.

  • Every property at once

    On hand, low items and open orders for each property, side by side.

  • Stock per place, and what to move

    Each item by place, and a move offered where one place is short and another has more.

  • Parts on a work order

    Their cost lands on the repair, and on the thing it fixed.

  • Parts installed in things

    A part's one life: in stock, installed, removed, repaired or disposed of.

  • Transfers suggested across properties

    Stock one property has to spare, offered where another runs low.

    Planned

How it works

A movement, every time.

  1. It is ordered

    Before it runs out, from its supplier, approved by an owner.

  2. It arrives

    Received against its order, at the invoice's cost.

  3. It is kept

    On a shelf in a place, counted when you count, moved where it is needed.

  4. It is sold or used

    By a sale, a department or a repair, and ordered again.

Who it is for

Anyone who keeps things on a shelf.

  • Hotels

    Linen, amenities and engineering's parts.

  • Restaurants and bars

    Food and drink, counted and costed.

  • Shops

    What is for sale, and what is left.

  • Service companies

    The parts every truck carries.
