Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 1, 2026

Our promises

Our promise. Oneway uses your information only to run Oneway, for example to choose what Home shows you. We never sell it, we never use it for personalized ads, and our team does not browse it. A person at Oneway sees information about you only when something is reported, when you ask us for help, or when the law requires it.

Oneway is new, so here is what you can count on, and where this policy shows how.

  • We never sell your information, and never use it for personalized ads. How
  • Our staff see only your email address and username, unless something is reported or flagged, you ask us for help, or the law requires it. Every look in our admin system is logged. How
  • Your messages are for the people in them. Moderators cannot open a conversation; they see only a message someone reported. How
  • Teens are protected from the first day: a private profile, messages only from people they follow, quiet nights, and a Home that is not ranked from what they do. How
  • Your location stays off until you turn it on, and we keep only your latest position. How
  • You can see, download and delete your data, in Settings: Understanding, Download your data, and Delete your account. How
  • A real person answers. Write to us about anything here. How

How we use it

Covers: Home, For you, Following, Discovery, Waypass, Settings

  • To run Oneway: sign you in, show your profile, deliver posts and messages, make bookings, and send the notifications you asked for.
  • To keep accounts safe: limit sign-in attempts, require two-step sign-in in casino, guest, rewards and credential areas, and keep the security log.
  • To rank Home from who and what you follow, the places you belong to, your reactions, what you have already seen and what you are watching now. None of this orders Home for a person under 18: their Home is the people and places they follow, newest first.
  • To order For you in two moments. When you open it, the first posts come from what you follow and belong to, places near yours, and updates from city and community Centers near you, with other people's reactions counting for little. As you scroll, posts that more people reacted to come first. A feed you set to top this week is ordered by reactions alone.
  • To favour posts from places near yours on Home. The places are the ones you belong to, follow, saved, visited or typed on your profile, and Oneway never reads your device's position for this. No more than one post in five comes from a nearby place you do not follow, and no author or place fills more than two of any ten. Following, and feeds you set to newest first, are not ranked.
  • To fill the feeds you build from the kinds of Center you choose, such as restaurants or hotels. Only the kinds you pick are used, and nothing is inferred about you from them.
  • To show friends what you share through Waypass: Centers you both belong to and events you are both going to.
  • To let places recognise you, if you allow it.
  • To moderate: an automatic check reads what people write before it shows, and people decide reports.
  • To write or suggest text and images when you use an AI feature.
  • To meet legal duties.

We do not read your messages to rank posts or show ads. Moderators cannot open conversations. People decide reports, and an automated check on a report can only change its place in the queue. Recognition only suggests, and a person at the business confirms.

Some automatic checks do act on their own, and each is described on this page: the word check refuses clear harm, holds words that could be harm and can move an account’s standing; a Marketplace listing that names something never sold here is refused; and a birthday under 13 pauses the account. Any refusal or hold can be taken to a person.

Workspace profiles

Covers: the OS, a Center's team, Settings under Workplace, workspace sign-up

A workspace profile is who you are at work, at the Centers where you work. It is kept apart from your personal profile.

  • What it holds. A work name and a job title, up to 80 characters each, and the posts you make on your workplace Centers.
  • Your coworkers (people who work at the same Center) see your work name and title, and which of their Centers you work at. Your role is never shown.
  • A Center's own list of its people shows your work name, with no title it did not give you and no @handle, so it cannot be joined to your personal profile.
  • Everyone else, signed out or not, sees only your personal name and handle. Nobody else is told where you work.
  • Use Workplace identity (Settings, Workplace; off until you turn it on): coworkers see your work profile, and your personal profile stays separate from them until you both follow each other.
  • Show me on public workplace Center pages (on at first): off hides you from those public pages. It does not remove you from the Center's internal team or employment records.
  • Signing up for a workspace, to join a Center's team or to start a business, asks which age range you are in, never your birthday.
  • A Center may also keep staff records, such as schedules and hours worked. Those are the Center's records.

The Marketplace

Covers: Marketplace listings, Message the seller, the listing chat

  • A listing keeps its photos, title, description, price or Free, category, condition, whether you take offers, and a meetup area.
  • The meetup area is a neighbourhood, never a street address. We keep its point only to about 1 km. The exact place and time are agreed in the chat and stay there, never on your profile.
  • Who sees a listing: people browsing near you, with your name and picture as your profile shows them. A person you blocked, or who blocked you, never sees your listings, and you never see theirs.
  • Message the seller opens one chat for each buyer and listing. That chat keeps its messages; it does not disappear after 2 days.
  • Scam warnings. The chat is read for requests to pay ahead (Zelle, Venmo, Cash App, a deposit or a gift card), for a code sent to a phone, or to ship only, and warns you in the chat.
  • Safe meetup spots are Centers that chose to offer themselves as public, staffed and lit places. The chat suggests them first.
  • Selling and messaging a seller are for people 18 and over. Under 18, you can browse only.
  • Paying. Today every listing is paid in person at pickup. Oneway does not handle the money. Paying through Oneway and shipping are not available on the Marketplace yet.

Paying a Center

Covers: buying from a Center, event tickets, booking and stay deposits

  • No account needed. When you buy from a Center through Oneway, you do not need an account.
  • Your card. You pay on Stripe’s own page, to the Center’s Stripe account. Your card details go to Stripe and never to Oneway, and Stripe keeps your email address, billing address and shipping address.
  • The order. We keep what it was for, how many, its amount, Oneway’s fee, the name Stripe was given, and whether Stripe says it is paid or refunded. If you were signed in, your account’s address is kept on the order too, so it is yours to find, and an event ticket answers you Going. A paid order is the Center’s record of a sale.
  • Deposits. A booking or a hotel stay may ask for a deposit. It is paid the same way, to the Center. A booking’s deposit is fixed when you book, so a later change to the Center’s rule does not change it.
  • Oneway’s fee is 1% of the sale or less. An order with a fee pays at least 10 cents, never more than the price of what was bought. The fee is given back in the same share as the sale is refunded, or lost in a dispute.
  • Disputes. If you dispute a payment with your bank, Stripe tells us. We keep the dispute's state on the order. While it is open, what you bought may be paused, for example a ticket put on hold.

Location, Waypass and Footsteps

Covers: the Discovery map, sharing your location, Waypass, Footsteps

  • Location sharing is off until you turn it on. You choose who: nobody, up to 25 people you name, followers, friends, people in your Centers, or everyone, including people not signed in. You choose how exact: exact, or about 1 km. You choose how long: 1 to 24 hours, or only while you use the App. We keep only your latest position, and turning sharing off deletes it.
  • Under 18, your location can be shared only with friends (people you follow who follow you back) or with people you name.
  • Waypass is off until a person 18 or over turns it on. While it is on and Oneway is open on your phone (or closed, only if you chose that), your phone’s position, to about 50 metres, and the second it was taken are held for about ten minutes to find people within about 150 metres of you at that moment, then deleted. A swap keeps who, the place by its name (a Center or a neighbourhood, never a point) and the time to about ten minutes, and you are both told at once a few minutes later. The other person sees your card: your name and picture as you share them. A block works both ways, and you can remove a swap, which takes you off each other’s lists for good.
  • Footsteps comes with Waypass. It keeps the square kilometres you have been in, never the exact spot or the time. Only you can see it, and you can clear it.

The Brain and AI

Covers: your Brain in Me, a Center’s Brain in the OS, AI features in Create and in a Center’s inbox

  • Your Brain is counted from your own activity, such as the Centers you belong to and what you post and save there. Only you see it, and it does not call an AI model.
  • A Center’s Brain reads that Center’s own records, such as follows, bookings, attendance and posts there, to see how people relate to the Center and to recommend what to do. A person at the Center decides. We do not infer health, religion, ethnicity, sexuality, politics or finances.
  • What a Center’s Brain may do by itself: only the small acts its owner allowed, up to a daily budget, each one recorded (and, if the owner asked, told to them). Larger acts wait for a person to approve. Acts that move money or touch a person’s identity are never the Brain’s to do alone.

Automatic moderation and account standing

Covers: posts, comments, captions, bios, a Center’s words, Marketplace listings, canvas text, messages, Shield

Everything people write on Oneway is read by an automatic check before it shows. It reads the whole sentence, whatever spelling hides the words, and it runs on our own servers: your words are not sent to anyone else to be checked.

  • Clear harm is refused everywhere, messages included: threats, slurs, sexual words beside a child or an age under 18, asking a child to keep secrets or to meet, and trading abuse material. You are told why and what to change. If we read it wrong, ask for a review and a person looks.
  • Words that could be harm are held in a post: only you see the post until you change it or a person reviews it. A post that gives someone’s home address or phone number as theirs is held the same way. A comment or a bio has nowhere to wait, so some words there are asked to be put another way.
  • Plain cursing shows. Cursing at a person is refused in public, and allowed in a message.
  • A message is never held. What is not clear harm is delivered, and the person who gets it can report it. A message is read together with the ones you sent just before it, so harm split across messages is still caught.
  • Without an account, a message to a Center’s team is checked more strictly, and is not sent if it has any of those words or a link: the page says so, so it can be written again.
  • Marketplace listings that name something never sold here are refused.
  • Account standing. Each account has one standing: normal, watched, held or locked. Three refusals in an hour, or three posts held, put an account under watch, and eight refusals in an hour hold it. Watched, words that would only be held in a comment, a bio or a message are refused. Held, every post waits for a person. Locked, nothing can be posted or sent for a time. Our team can also restrict an account or change its standing, with a reason. Every move has an end and is written to the security log.
  • Shield. While Oneway is under attack, our team can switch on Shield for a while: posts and links from accounts less than a day old wait for a person, and so do posts from an account whose email is not confirmed. A Center’s managers can switch on their own Shield for their pages and comments. Every change has a reason and an end, and is written to the security log.
  • Reports are reviewed by people.

Edited posts

Covers: editing a post you made in Create or Home

An edited post shows Edited. Its original, the post as others first saw it, is kept, with up to 10 earlier versions, for moderation; a draft nobody else has seen has no original. The original is shown only to its author, unless the author chooses Show the original to readers on that post; then readers can open it, marked as the original, while its words pass the same automatic moderation a post does and no removal request or review holds the post. Oneway’s reviewers can read a post’s history when a case needs it; a Center’s own case view never shows it.

Removal requests and child safety

Covers:,, every photo and video upload

  • Intimate pictures shared without consent. Under the TAKE IT DOWN Act, the person shown, or someone acting for them, can ask us to remove an intimate picture shared without their consent. We keep the request: name, email, where the picture is, the statement and the typed signature. A person at Oneway reviews it within 48 hours. The person who posted it is never told who asked. We also keep a fingerprint (a hash) of the removed file, so the same file cannot be stored again.
  • Copyright notices. We keep the notice. The person who posted is shown the claimant’s name and what they claim, never their address or phone.
  • Child sexual abuse material is never allowed. Matching photos and videos against lists of known abuse material needs approval from the service that keeps those lists, which Oneway does not have yet, so that check is not running. When it runs, a match is never shown: it is kept locked as evidence, the account is held, and a person at Oneway files a report with the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC). We keep that record for one year.
    The law: 18 U.S.C. 2258A requires a provider to report apparent child sexual abuse material to NCMEC’s CyberTipline, and to preserve the report’s contents for one year. In plain words: we report it, and we keep the evidence for the authorities.

Security

  • Passwords are kept as bcrypt hashes and session tokens as SHA-256 hashes: scrambled one way, so they cannot be turned back into the password or token. The sign-in cookie cannot be read by scripts on a page and is sent only to our own site.
  • These are encrypted with AES-256-GCM, a strong standard cipher, with a separate key for each business or person and a master key kept outside the database: connected-account access, linked mail sign-ins, two-step secrets, and casino ID and taxpayer numbers.
  • Sign-ins and changes to who can get in are written to a log built so that any later change to it can be detected.
  • Other records, such as posts, profiles and bookings, are ordinary database records. We will state the hosting and disk encryption here before the App opens to the public.
  • No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your private information, we will tell you and the authorities as New York and other state laws require. Report a security problem to hello@oneway.center. The Security page says more.

Teens and children

Covers: sign-up, every protection a teen account starts with, parent supervision

Oneway is for people 13 and older. When you sign up for the App we ask your birthday. It stays private, is never shown on your profile, and sets the protections for your age. When you sign up for a workspace, to join a Center’s team or to start a business, we ask only which age range you are in, never your birthday.

A sign-up from someone younger than 13 is refused before anything about them is stored, and we do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 13. If an account is later given a birthday under 13, we pause it at once: nobody can see it and nothing new is collected from it. If that birthday is wrong, email us at hello@oneway.center from the account’s address within 30 days and someone at Oneway will check the age and turn the account back on; otherwise the account and everything in it is deleted. If you think a child under 13 has an account, email us and we will delete it.

The law: the Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA, 15 U.S.C. 6501 to 6506) says that a service which knows a user is under 13 needs a parent’s verifiable consent before it collects that child’s personal information. In plain words: we do not let children under 13 have accounts, and we pause and then delete any account we learn belongs to one.

An account for someone 13 to 17 starts protected:

  • The profile is private until they choose otherwise.
  • Only people they follow can message them.
  • Notifications are held from 10 PM to 7 AM in their own time zone, and from midnight to 6 AM Eastern whatever their time zone.
  • Their Home is the people and places they follow, newest first. It is not ordered from what they react to, save or watch.
  • After 60 minutes in a day the App says so once and goes on working; they can choose to keep going, and a weekly recap shows the days they did.
  • Their location can be shared only with friends or people they name. Waypass, and selling or messaging on the Marketplace, are for people 18 and over.
  • They can invite a parent or guardian with an adult account to supervise. A parent can make the quiet hours longer and the day shorter, answers a young teen’s request to open their profile, and sees who they talk to by name and never what they say. Either of them can end it.
  • From 13 to 15, their posts are not recommended to people who do not follow them, and their profile can be opened to everyone only with a parent’s approval.

Where a state’s law asks for more for minors, that law applies. We have read New York’s SAFE for Kids Act and Child Data Protection Act, and we apply the strictest rule we have read to every account under 18, in every state, until we have read that state’s own law.

Your birthday

Covers: App sign-up, the birthday question at sign-in, Settings

  • We say it back first. Before any birthday is saved, we show it in words with the age it makes, for example “March 2, 2010, which makes you 16”, and you press again to confirm. Every date gets the same check, so it never hints at which age is wanted.
  • Accounts made before we asked are asked their birthday once, at sign-in.
  • You can change it once, in Settings, after we ask whether you are sure. Every change is written to the security log.
  • In some states a person at Oneway reviews the change first. In Texas, every change. In Tennessee and Mississippi, a change that makes you 18 or over. In Georgia, a change that makes you 16 or over. We go by the strictest of what you tell us, your network and the state we noted for you earlier.
    The law: Texas (the SCOPE Act, section 509.051(c)) lets a registered age be changed only through a commercially reasonable review. Tennessee (HB 1891), Mississippi (HB 1126) and Georgia (SB 351) require a checked age, and a parent’s consent for a minor. Letting someone just type a new birthday would get around both. In plain words: in those states, a person checks before your age goes up.
  • The review asks for a photo of an ID. It is sealed, shown only to the person at Oneway who reviews it, and deleted when they decide, or after 14 days if nobody has. Tennessee’s law says data used to check an age may not be kept, and we keep it no longer than the review.
