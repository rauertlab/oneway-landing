Our promises

Oneway is new, so here is what you can count on, and where this page shows how.

  • We never see your password. It is stored scrambled one way, so it cannot be read back. How
  • Two-step verification is there for every account, with codes from an authenticator app, never by text message. How
  • Our staff see only your email address and username, unless something is reported or flagged, and every look is logged. How
  • One business on Oneway can never reach another’s data, and an automated test checks it for every endpoint. How
  • Secrets are write-only. Passwords for the systems you or a business connect are locked away, and no screen or endpoint shows them back. How
  • You stay in control of who can reach you and where you are signed in, in Settings. How
  • A real person answers, and you can report a problem or a vulnerability. How
  • We claim no certification we do not hold, and we mark what is not built yet. How

Signing in and sessions

Covers: sign-up, sign-in, Settings (password, two-step sign-in, sessions), the OS

  • Passwords are stored as bcrypt hashes (work factor 12), each with its own salt. A hash can check a password but cannot be turned back into it. A password is never stored, logged or returned in plain text.
  • Two-step sign-in uses six-digit codes from any standard authenticator app (TOTP, RFC 6238). Codes are checked in constant time, so timing gives nothing away, and recovery codes are stored only as hashes. We do not send codes by text message, because SIM swapping defeats them.
  • Two-step sign-in is available on every account and required where the stakes are highest: the whole operating side of a casino’s Center asks for it by default.
  • Sessions are kept on our servers. Your browser holds only a random token, in a __Host- cookie marked Secure, HttpOnly and SameSite=Lax. In plain words: it is sent only over HTTPS, only to Oneway, no script on a page can read it, and other sites cannot make your browser use it for a change. Signing out ends the session on the server, not only in the browser.
  • Changing your password ends every other session on the account at once.
  • Signing in reveals no account. Signing in or resetting a password while signed out gives the same answer whether or not an address has an account.
  • Sign-in limits. Failed sign-ins are counted two ways. From one network address: by default 12 in five minutes, then sign-in from there waits until the five minutes pass. For one account, from any network: by default 10 in 15 minutes, then that account’s sign-in waits. The answer is the same whether or not the account exists, and a successful sign-in clears the account’s count.
  • Rate limits on every endpoint, per session and per network address, so one person’s traffic never uses up another’s. Over the limit, a request is answered with 429 and a Retry-After, and nothing behind the endpoint runs.

Between people and between Centers

Covers: a Center’s operating side in the OS, its roles, every Center endpoint

  • Authority comes from the relationship, never from the request. What a person may see or do is decided by their real standing with a Center (owner, admin, staff or member) and by what each person has chosen to share, never by an identifier the caller supplies.
  • One Center can never reach another's operating side. An automated test reads every Center endpoint from the application's own route list and calls each one as the owner of a different Center. A new endpoint is tested the day it is added.
  • Every change requires a signed-in person. An automated test calls every endpoint that changes anything while signed out; none succeeds, except the few a signed-out person needs, such as signing in and resetting a password.
  • Roles each Center defines for itself. Beyond owner, admin and staff, a Center can name its own roles. A role can only narrow what a member of staff may see and do, and every role change is written to the security log.
  • Workspace profiles stay apart. A person’s work name is shown with no @handle to anyone who is not a coworker, so it cannot be joined to their personal profile.

What Oneway’s staff can see

Covers: Oneway’s admin system

  • The list of accounts shows only each account’s email address and username, beside Oneway’s own notes on it (restricted, reported, its standing).
  • More opens only for an account that was reported or flagged, and only what the case needs: the reported item and why, the account’s age and recent counts. Never a private message beyond the one reported, never who reported, never an address it signed in from.
  • Every view and every search is written to the security log first. A view that cannot be logged is not shown.
  • Only Oneway’s owner, and staff the owner gives this permission, can use it. Restricting an account asks for a reason, and for the staff member’s own password and two-step code, and an admin can never restrict the owner or another staff member from there.
  • Child-safety records are read only by a named person who holds that one permission, and never through the platform’s key.

Encryption and secrets

Covers: a Center’s integrations, connected accounts, linked mail accounts, two-step sign-in, a casino’s ID records

  • Every Center has its own secrets vault. Credentials a Center uses to connect its own systems are encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a data key that belongs to that Center alone.
  • A person’s secrets have their own vault too, such as the sign-in of each mail account they link. It is never shown back.
  • Envelope encryption. Each vault’s data key is itself encrypted under a master key that is never stored in the database.
  • Every encrypted value is bound to its place. Each one is sealed together with its owner and its name (authenticated encryption, AEAD). A value copied to another Center, or renamed, fails to open, and any changed byte fails the check.
  • Secrets are write-only. No endpoint returns a secret, not even to its owner. Operators see a secret’s name, who set it, when, and when an integration last used it. The value reaches only the server code that calls that provider.
  • Keys are 256-bit values from the operating system’s secure random generator, with a fresh nonce for every encryption.
  • Key rotation without exposure. Rotating the master key re-wraps each data key. No secret is decrypted to do it.
  • Crypto-shredding. Removing a Center’s vault destroys its data key, and from that moment the platform can no longer open any secret it held. Encrypted database backups made before the removal still hold the old key until they age out, within 14 days.
  • Personal secrets, such as a two-step seed, are sealed under a key made for that one purpose and bound to the person they belong to.
  • In transit: HTTPS everywhere, with HTTP Strict Transport Security for one year across every subdomain.

Integrations and apps

Covers: Connected accounts, a Center’s integrations and feeds, Popit apps, widgets

  • A business connects its own systems. A provider cannot sign itself in to a Center. Each Center sets up its own integrations, with credentials only it holds, kept in its own vault.
  • Every connection asks first. Before another platform’s sign-in screen, Oneway lists each permission and why, and the server refuses to connect until each one is agreed.
  • Every feed has its own key, shown once when it is issued and stored only as a SHA-256 hash. Issuing a new key disables the old one at once.
  • Signed webhooks. Messages from a connected vendor are checked by their HMAC-SHA256 signature, in constant time, before anything is read, and a signed message is refused once it is too old to be fresh.
  • Apps built on Oneway, including Popit apps, run on their own sandbox host, under their own content security policy, with only the permissions a person allowed.
  • Widgets use a token made for one device that can only read its owner’s Home. It is stored as a hash, signs nobody in, and ends with the session that made it.

Outbound requests

Covers: integrations, linked mail accounts, any fetch Oneway’s servers make

Server-side request forgery is when a server is tricked into fetching an address that points back inside its own network. It is how a vendor’s server becomes a way into its customers’ networks. Every request an integration makes goes through one guarded fetcher:

  • HTTPS only, to the hosts each integration is allowed to reach.
  • Public internet addresses only. Addresses inside a network (private, loopback, link-local, carrier-grade NAT and reserved ranges), cloud metadata endpoints, and their IPv6 and IPv4-mapped forms are all refused. Mail connections are checked the same way.
  • The name is looked up once, and the connection is made to exactly the address that was checked. So a name that changes its answer after the check (DNS rebinding) cannot send the request somewhere else.
  • TLS is verified against the host name, and every redirect is checked again from the start.
  • Every request is bounded in size and time, and no proxy is taken from the environment.

In the browser

Covers: every Oneway page

  • X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff: a response is never read as a type other than the one it declares.
  • frame-ancestors 'self': no other site can frame Oneway’s pages to trick you into clicking.
  • Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin: no path or query string leaks to another site.
  • A Permissions-Policy that allows location, the camera and the microphone only to Oneway’s own pages, each only when you allow it, and shuts Bluetooth, USB, serial ports, HID devices and payment.
  • Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin, and Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy: same-site on data, so another site cannot embed Oneway’s data.
  • A video player from another platform runs in a sandboxed frame and is sent no referrer.

Files and photos

Covers: every upload: posts, messages, profile and Center pictures, listings, stickers

  • A file is identified by its bytes (its magic number), never by its name or its declared type. Anything unrecognized is refused.
  • Sizes are capped before anything is decoded.
  • Photos are stored without where they were taken or by what. GPS position, device and capture data are removed before storage, without changing a pixel, and the orientation is kept.
  • Content-addressed storage. Every file is stored under its owner and a SHA-256 digest of its contents. An upload cannot choose its path, climb out of its folder or overwrite anyone else’s file.
  • The child-safety check is not running yet. Matching photos and videos against lists of known child sexual abuse material needs approval from the service that keeps those lists, which Oneway does not have yet. When it runs, every photo and video passes one writer that checks it before it is stored; if the service cannot answer, the upload is refused, never stored unchecked; and a match is kept locked as evidence that no page serves, the account is held, and a person at Oneway reports it to NCMEC.

Moderation, account standing and Shield

Covers: everything people write, Report, Oneway’s admin system, a Center’s own Shield

  • Every word is read before it shows, in posts, comments, bios, listings, canvas text and messages, whatever spelling hides it: look-alike letters, numbers for letters, stretched or spaced letters, emoji standing in for words, and masks such as f*ck. It matches whole words, so an ordinary word that contains a short bad one is never caught. It runs on our own servers, with no outside service.
  • Clear harm is refused, and words that could be harm hold a post for a person to review. Each answer says what to change, and any refusal or hold can be taken to a person.
  • One standing for each account: normal, watched, held or locked, read once on every write. Three refusals in an hour put an account under watch, and eight hold it. Every move has a reason and an end, is written to the security log, and can be lifted or extended from Oneway’s admin system.
  • Shield is a set of stricter rules for a raid, each its own switch with a reason and an end, all off with one press: posts and links from new accounts wait for a person, and so do posts from an account whose email is not confirmed. A Center’s managers have their own Shield for its pages and comments. Every change is logged.
  • Reports are reviewed by people. A case shows the reported item and why, never who reported.

The security log

Covers: Settings (your security page), a Center’s security log in the OS, Oneway’s admin system

  • A tamper-evident security log records sign-ins, refused sign-ins, changes to how an account is protected, birthday changes, changes to roles and access, and every view in Oneway’s admin system. Each entry is chained to the one before it with an HMAC, so an altered or removed entry shows exactly where. It never records a password, a code, a token or a credential’s value.
  • Dependencies are pinned to exact versions with cryptographic hashes, on a supported runtime.

Talk to a person, report a problem

Covers: everything on this page

  • Write to hello@oneway.center. A person at Oneway reads and answers.
  • Think someone else is in your account? Change your password (every other session ends at once), check Where you are signed in, and write to us.
  • Something wrong on Oneway? Press Report on the post, comment, person, Center or listing. A person reviews every report.

Vulnerability reports are welcome at hello@oneway.center; our security.txt (RFC 9116) says where to send them. We read every report. Give us a reasonable time to fix it, and do not read, change or keep other people’s data to prove it. We will not take action against good-faith research that follows the Terms.

The standard we hold ourselves to

The bar named at the top of this page, the one the most regulated industry we serve sets for its vendors, is the standard we are building toward: OWASP ASVS Level 3 (the strictest level of a public checklist for web application security) for every endpoint, mapped to a recognized control framework (CIS Controls, ISO/IEC 27001 or NIST SP 800-53), and GLI-GSF-3, the gaming industry’s information security audit for vendors. A SOC 2 Type II report and independent penetration testing are part of that program. This page will say when each is complete, and never before.
